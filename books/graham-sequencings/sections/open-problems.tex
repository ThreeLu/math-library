\section{Open problems}\label{sec:open}

Each problem is stated in its algebraic form and then, through Propositions~\ref{prop:whole} and~\ref{prop:subsets}, as a problem about rainbow paths in Cayley digraphs.

\begin{conjecture}[Keedwell, 1981; Ollis~\cite{oll25}, Conjecture~15]\label{conj:keedwell}
Every finite non-abelian group other than $D_6$, $D_8$ and $Q_8$ is sequenceable.
\end{conjecture}

By Proposition~\ref{prop:whole}(i), this says that for every finite non-abelian group $G\notin\{D_6,D_8,Q_8\}$ the digraph $\operatorname{Cay}(G,G\setminus\{e\})$ contains a rainbow directed Hamilton path. It is known for all non-abelian groups of order $10$ to $255$, for dihedral groups of order at least $10$, for soluble groups with a unique involution except $Q_8$, and for every sufficiently large non-abelian group~\cite{mp22}, \cite[\S2.6]{oll25}. Open are the non-abelian groups of order at least $256$ outside these families and below the inexplicit threshold, of which M\"uyesser remarks that ``$10^{50}$ or so should be comfortably enough''~\cite[\S2.5]{oll25}.

\begin{conjecture}[Graham~\cite{gra71}]\label{conj:graham-every}
For every prime $p$, every $S\subseteq\Z_p\setminus\{0\}$ has an ordering $s_1,\dots,s_k$ with $s_1,\ s_1+s_2,\ \dots,\ s_1+\dots+s_k$ distinct.
\end{conjecture}

By Proposition~\ref{prop:subsets}(i): for every prime $p$ and every $S\subseteq\Z_p\setminus\{0\}$, the digraph $\operatorname{Cay}(\Z_p,S)$ contains a rainbow directed path with $|S|-1$ arcs. This is known for all $p\geq p_0$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:graham-large}); for every $p$ when $|S|\leq12$ or $|S|\geq p-3$~\cite{hos19,cdor22}; and for $p\leq23$, from the computer check of Conjecture~\ref{conj:adms} for $n\leq25$~\cite[\S1]{hos19}. Open are the primes $29\leq p<p_0$ with $13\leq|S|\leq p-4$; no explicit $p_0$ is known.

\begin{conjecture}[Archdeacon--Dinitz--Mattern--Stinson~\cite{admc16}]\label{conj:adms-open}
For every $n$, every $A\subseteq\Z_n\setminus\{0\}$ has an ordering $a_1,\dots,a_k$ whose partial sums $s_1,\dots,s_k$ are distinct.
\end{conjecture}

By Proposition~\ref{prop:subsets}(i): for every $n$ and every $A\subseteq\Z_n\setminus\{0\}$, the digraph $\operatorname{Cay}(\Z_n,A)$ contains a rainbow directed path with $|A|-1$ arcs. It is known for $n\leq25$ by computer and for $|A|\leq6$~\cite[\S1]{hos19}; for $n=tp$ with $\Z_t$ strongly sequenceable and $p$ a large prime; and for $n$ whose prime factors are large and comparable, with bounded total multiplicity~\cite{cdfl26}. It would give a simple ordering of every row and column of every Heffter array, and hence their cycle decompositions.

\begin{conjecture}[Alspach; see Bode--Harborth~\cite{bh05}]\label{conj:alspach-open}
For every $n$, every $A\subseteq\Z_n\setminus\{0\}$ with $\sum_{a\in A}a\neq0$ has an ordering whose partial sums $0,s_1,\dots,s_k$ are distinct.
\end{conjecture}

By Proposition~\ref{prop:subsets}(ii): for every such $n$ and $A$, the digraph $\operatorname{Cay}(\Z_n,A)$ contains a rainbow directed path with $|A|$ arcs, using every colour. It is known for $|A|\in\{n-1,n-2\}$, and claimed for $|A|\leq5$ and for $n\leq16$~\cite{bh05}; for prime $n$ it is known for $|A|\leq10$ and $|A|\geq n-3$~\cite{hos19}; and it holds for $|A|\leq\exp(c(\log p)^{1/3})$, where $p$ is the least prime divisor of $n$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:cdf}). It implies Conjecture~\ref{conj:adms-open}~\cite[Proposition~1.1]{admc16}.

\begin{conjecture}[Ollis~\cite{oll25}, Conjecture~38; a question of Alspach and Kalinowski, see Alspach--Liversidge~\cite{al20}]\label{conj:strongly}
In every finite abelian group $G$, every $S\subseteq G\setminus\{0\}$ has an ordering whose partial sums $0,s_1,\dots,s_k$ are distinct, or are distinct except that $s_k=0$.
\end{conjecture}

By Proposition~\ref{prop:subsets}(ii) and (iii): for every finite abelian group $G$ and every $S\subseteq G\setminus\{0\}$, the digraph $\operatorname{Cay}(G,S)$ contains a rainbow directed path with $|S|$ arcs or a rainbow directed cycle with $|S|$ arcs. It is known for $|S|\leq9$~\cite{al20}, and for further small $|S|$ under conditions on the group~\cite[Theorem~39]{oll25}. For cyclic groups it implies Conjectures~\ref{conj:adms-open} and~\ref{conj:alspach-open}.

\begin{problem}[Bedert et al.~\cite{bbkmm25}, Question~1.1]\label{prob:all-groups}
For which finite groups $G$ does every $S\subseteq G\setminus\{e\}$ have an ordering $s_1,\dots,s_k$ with $s_1,\ s_1s_2,\ \dots,\ s_1\cdots s_k$ distinct? Equivalently, for which $G$ does $\operatorname{Cay}(G,S)$ contain a rainbow directed path with $|S|-1$ arcs for every $S\subseteq G\setminus\{e\}$?
\end{problem}

The answer is yes for every $S$ with $|S|\geq|G|^{1-c}$ in every group, and for $\mathbb F_2^n$ when $|S|\geq C$~\cite{bbkmm25}. The authors write that ``it seems feasible that the answer \dots\ is affirmative for every finite group''.

\begin{problem}[Ollis~\cite{oll25}, \S3.6]\label{prob:row-complete-prime}
Is there a row-complete Latin square of prime order $p\geq13$? Equivalently, is there a Williams design for $p$ treatments with a single square? In graph form: can the complete digraph on $p$ vertices be decomposed into $p$ directed Hamilton paths $P_1,\dots,P_p$ such that, for each $j$, the $j$-th vertices of $P_1,\dots,P_p$ are all different?
\end{problem}

Row-complete Latin squares exist for every even order, and for every odd composite order~\cite{hig98}; there are none of order $3$, $5$, $7$ or $11$ (Best and Wanless, reported in~\cite[\S3.6]{oll25}). Groups cannot help here: the only group of order $p$ is $\Z_p$, which has no involution and so is not sequenceable.

\begin{problem}[Buci\'c et al.~\cite{bfmpy25}, Problem~1.6]\label{prob:directed-schrijver}
Let $D$ be a $d$-regular digraph whose arcs are properly coloured with $d$ colours. Does $D$ contain a rainbow directed path with $d-1$ arcs?
\end{problem}

Specialising to $D=\operatorname{Cay}(G,S)$ with $d=|S|$, a positive answer would give, in every finite group, an ordering of every $S\subseteq G\setminus\{e\}$ with distinct partial products, settling Conjecture~\ref{conj:graham-every}, Conjecture~\ref{conj:adms-open} and Problem~\ref{prob:all-groups} at once~\cite[\S1]{bfmpy25}. Rainbow paths with $(1-o(1))d$ arcs exist when $d=\Omega(n)$~\cite[Theorem~1.7]{bfmpy25}; the problem is solved asymptotically for complete symmetric digraphs~\cite{bps20}; and the undirected Schrijver question has an answer with $(1-o(1))d$ edges~\cite[Theorem~1.5]{bfmpy25}.

\topic{Summary}

Experiments and dances (Lucas 1883, Williams 1949) ask for row-complete Latin squares. Gordon (1961) showed that a sequenceable group gives one, so which groups are sequenceable is a question about the group. Graham (1971), Archdeacon et al.\ (Heffter arrays) and Alspach (cycle decompositions) ask the same for subsets. The translation is that distinct partial products are rainbow paths in the Cayley digraph, and the group enters only through the colour-preserving translations.
