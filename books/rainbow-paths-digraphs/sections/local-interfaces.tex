\section{Local interfaces and fractional completion}
\label{rsg:section}\label{rpf:section}\label{sg:sec:inputs}

Every selected configuration and stored resource set is physically
admissible. Ambient source and target classes may contain both copies,
with physical labels injective on each side. A selected configuration
uses at most one copy of each label; avoiding a label excludes both.
Write $L=\log(2m)$ and fix $\beta=L^{-12000},g=\lceil{}mL^{-12600}\rceil,b=\lceil{}mL^{-12500}\rceil$. Fixed positive multiples of $g,b$ are equally permissible.

For the next proposition, fix $p_0>0$, $0\le\rho<1$ and $\eta>0$. Let $H[X,Y]$ be simple and properly $D$-coloured, with no edge joining equal physical labels, and put $m=\max(|X|,|Y|)$. Assume $D\ge{}p_0m$, $d_H(z)\in[D-\varepsilon_0m,D]$, and $|M_c|\ge(1-\varepsilon_0)m$ \refstepcounter{equation}\label{rsg:partial-hypotheses}\textup{(\ref{rsg:partial-hypotheses})}. Zero-pad the smaller class to obtain $A$ and require $\|A-(D/m)J_m\|\le\rho{}D$ \refstepcounter{equation}\label{rsg:gap-hypothesis}\textup{(\ref{rsg:gap-hypothesis})}.

Addition queries have disjoint admissible external inputs of $a$ vertices per side and $c$ colours, $a,c\le{}m^{4/5}$. Stored and input palettes avoid their identity colour and its fixed finite exceptional set. An ordinary query may prescribe any missing head $y\in{}Y$; its identity colour has a real predecessor of $y$ outside the forbidden resources. This root edge may be impermissible and is never an output identity.

For a small fixed core rate $\kappa>0$, put $v_0=\kappa{}m$ and sample source, target and colour roles at rates $v_0/|X|,v_0/|Y|,v_0/D$, with mutually exclusive physical-copy roles. The certificate refers to anchored degrees, six upper shadows and the incidence Gram gap. Its small-extension tolerance depends only on density and gap; the final error threshold and order bound may also depend on the chosen $\kappa$.

\begin{proposition}[Robust local singular-gap interfaces]\label{rsg:proposition}\label{rpf:proposition}
For sufficiently small $\varepsilon_0$ and large $m$, the following interfaces exist.
\begin{enumerate}
\item At least $(1-\eta)D$ identity colours have pools of at least $(1-\eta)m$ real edges satisfying Theorem~\ref{rpe:theorem}.
\item For fixed sufficiently large $K=K(p_0,\rho)$, ordinary and spare addition modules have sizes $(2g+1,g+1)$ and $(2g,g+K)$, recording side and palette sizes. They output $g+a-c$ permitted identity edges and $g+c$ rainbow edges. The ordinary output misses one vertex per side, with prescribed head; the spare covers all vertices.
\item Protected ordinary modules have side and palette size $B_0=b+g+1$; protected spares have side size $B_1=b+g$ and palette size $B_1+K$. Any fixed number obey the sequential reservation rule below.
\item With probability tending to one, the balanced sampled core has the stated certificate, surviving $o(m)$ protected changes. Every balanced target within its extension tolerance has a fractional perfect matching and a matching missing $O(m^{2/3}\log^4m)$ vertices per part.
\end{enumerate}
Interiors avoid the inverse-logarithmic forbidden populations below. After further deletions, the interfaces apply whenever the same hypotheses hold for the residual graph and palette. Completed edges and dummy copies are never output resources.
\end{proposition}

We prove the local assertions first, then the fractional certificate. In this proof write $\varepsilon=\varepsilon_0$. Choose $\rho<\rho_1<1$, fixed lengths $r,K$ as below, and a small physical-alias threshold $\varepsilon_*$.  Next choose a candidate loss $\eta_1<\eta$ so small that $O_{p_0,r,K}(\eta_1)\ll1$ in every finite configuration family, and invoke Theorem~\ref{rpe:theorem}
with loss $\eta_1$.  Finally choose $\varepsilon_0$ for that theorem, all finite-word rejection bounds, and the eventual fractional
threshold.  Thus fixed gadget lengths precede both error parameters.

\subsection{Real alternating supports}
\label{sg:lem:C4}\label{sg:sec:C7}

Every colour matching can be completed between two padded $m$-sets. Write their sum as $\widetilde{A}=A+B$.  If rows were padded, every real row of $B$ has sum at most $\varepsilon{}m$, every dummy row at most $D$, and every column at most $\varepsilon{}m$; hence \refstepcounter{equation}\label{rsg:completion-gap}$\|B\|\le\sqrt{D\varepsilon{}m},\|\widetilde{A}-(D/m)J_m\|\le\rho_1D$ \textup{(\ref{rsg:completion-gap})}. Transpose for padded columns.  In a completed random word, stop at
the first artificial transition.  Until then the current copy is
real, with at most $\varepsilon{}m$ bad colours, so a length-$h$ word fails this way with probability at most $h\varepsilon{}m/D$. No estimate after entry into a dummy copy is used.  After its first
real uniform-colour step, a real-prefix subprobability law has atoms
at most $1/D$.  Real averaged colour maps are doubly substochastic and prescribed real matching maps are partial injections, preserving
this bound.  Missing identity endpoints and impermissible identity
pairs therefore cost $O_{p_0,h}(\varepsilon+\eta_1)$ at fixed positions.

Fix a candidate identity colour $c_0$, its real involution $J$, and a completed permutation matrix $S$.  The following three supports use only permitted real identity edges.  Roots in the first support
are allowed to be arbitrary distinct physical labels.

\paragraph{Endpoint support (C4).}
There are linearly many configurations, disjoint in free physical
vertices and free colours, with $r-1$ internal identity edges and an $r$-edge rainbow matching on their vertices and two prescribed opposite-side roots.  To count them, retain real end adjacency
factors in $AS^{\mathsf{T}}\widetilde{A}S^{\mathsf{T}}\cdots{}S^{\mathsf{T}}\widetilde{A}S^{\mathsf{T}}A$. Its constant contribution is at least $D^{r-2}(D-\varepsilon{}m)^2/m$; the centred contribution has absolute value at most $\rho_1^{r-2}D^{r-1}$, since the real end row and column have norms at most $\sqrt{D}$.  Choose fixed $r\ge3$ so $\rho_1^{r-2}<p_0/4$.  Dividing by $D^{r-1}$ leaves a fixed positive closing probability after the preceding real-edge and
permitted-pair rejections.

Each fresh letter excludes $O_r(1)$ choices for old physical labels, roots, repeated colours and $c_0$.  A closing colour equal to an earlier nonfinal letter is a forced-final-letter event.  If it equals
the final growth colour $t$, fixing $t$ forces the preceding endpoint; that endpoint has atom at most $1/D$, because at least one earlier random step occurred.  Averaging over $t$ gives the same $O(1/D)$ bound.  Thus collision rejection costs $O_r(1/D)$. A fixed free vertex or colour has load $O_r(D^{r-2})$: specify its position and all other letters, then solve the remaining injective
real transition; a specified closing colour similarly pins the last
letter.  Greedy packing from $\Omega(D^{r-1})$ configurations gives the claimed linear disjoint supply.

\paragraph{One-colour support (C5).}
For $c\ne{}c_0$ let $b_J(c)$ count real words $c_0,c,c_0$ starting in the head class whose endpoint physical labels coincide.
Reversal bijects these bad words with the bad words starting in
the source class, so the same exceptional set works for both. Put $E_J=\{c:b_J(c)>\varepsilon_*m\},\sum_cb_J(c)\le{}m,|E_J|\le\varepsilon_*^{-1}$. The sum bound holds because each start determines at most one bad
colour.  This endpoint equality is the only physical collision in
the four-copy word.  For $c\notin{}E_J\cup\{c_0\}$, undefined or impermissible steps remove $O(\varepsilon+\eta_1)m$ starts, and aliases remove at most $\varepsilon_*m$.  Close its endpoints using C4, avoiding its vertices and $c$.  The result has $r+1$ identity edges and a rainbow perfect matching of size $r+1$ containing $c$, with $r$ free colours.  Bounded loads and greedy packing again give a linear disjoint supply.

\paragraph{Prescribed-palette support (C6).}
For each fixed $j$ and palette $T$ of at least $5j$ colours avoiding $c_0$, there are linearly many disjoint sets with $j+1$ identity edges and a $T$-rainbow matching of size $j$.  Restrict to $5j$ colours.  Among all words of length at most $2j+1$ in $T\cup\{c_0\}$, at most $C_j(\varepsilon+\eta_1)m$ starts encounter a missing or impermissible transition, by partial injectivity.  At another start
extend $c_0,t_1,c_0,\ldots,t_i,c_0$.  If its endpoint is $u$ and its old tail and head sets are $A_i,B_i$, exclude for the next tail $I_tu$ the sets $A_i\cup{}J(B_i)\cup\iota(B_i)\cup{}J\iota(A_i)$. Together with the $i$ used colours these exclude at most $5i+4<5j$ choices.  The new identity pair is internally admissible by looplessness.  A bounded-radius packing in the graph of these
fixed colours and physical identifications gives the linear family.
The case $j=0$ is just a permitted identity edge.

These supports can be sampled into smaller roles.  In each packed
family, free vertices and colours are disjoint, so survival trials
are independent.  With source/head role rate $s$ and free-colour rate $q$, the respective probabilities are $s^{2r-2}q^r$, $s^{2(r+1)}q^r$ and $s^{2j+2}$, anchoring the roots and prescribed colours.  Chernoff and a union bound over
the polynomially many fixed-size queries apply also when $s,q$ are fixed inverse powers of $L$: every supply is $m/L^{O(1)}$. Each changed resource damages at most one packed configuration.
Delete forbidden and impermissible pairs at a fixed positive support
rate first; only then take tiny samples.  Thus the fixed loss $\eta_1m$ is never charged against an inverse-logarithmic supply.

\subsection{Basic addition and a prescribed missing head}
\label{sg:lem:basic}\label{sg:lem:addition}
\label{rphys:section}\label{rphys:module}
\label{sec:rooted-ordinary-checkpoint}

Set $S_0=r^2$, $H_0=S_0+r+2$, and take $K\ge5(S_0+1)$, enlarged for the rooted construction below. All stored and input palettes here avoid $E_J\cup\{c_0\}$.

\paragraph{The basic module.}
Take tiny disjoint supports $V_0,C_0$, with larger complementary supports $V_1,C_1$.  Store every supported permitted identity edge in $V_0$, forming $M_1$ of size $u$.  For every $c\in{}C_0$, use C5 to store a disjoint block in $V_1$ with identity matching $E_c$ and rainbow matching $F_c$, both of size $r+1$, whose other colours are fresh in $C_1$.  Put $M_2=\bigcup_cF_c$, $v=(r+1)|C_0|$, and pad its palette to $v+K$.  The entire permitted identity pool is $M_1\cup\bigcup_cE_c$.

For an external input $(a,a,c)$ maximize $(S_0+1)a'+R$ over $a'\le{}a$, $-S_0a'\le{}R\le{}c$, and matchings on exactly the stored vertices and $a'$ external vertices per side, of identity and rainbow sizes $u+a'-R,v+R$, subject to original-edge damage $|M_i\setminus{}M_i'|\le{}H_0[(S_0+1)a'+R](i=1,2)$ \textup{(A.1)}. The original pair is feasible.  Call $c\in{}C_0$ good when its entire original $F_c$ remains.  If external roots $x,y$ remain, choose C4 with $r-1$ undamaged identity edges and $r$ good colours. Remove these identity edges and insert the corresponding $r$ blocks $E_c$; replace those $r$ blocks $F_c$ by the endpoint rainbow matching.  Then $(a',R)\mapsto(a'+1,R-S_0),\Delta|M_1'|=r(r+1)-(r-1)=S_0+1,\Delta|M_2'|=-S_0$. At most $r-1$ original identity and $S_0+r$ original rainbow edges are newly lost.  The objective increases by one, and its
damage allowance increases by $H_0>S_0+r$, a contradiction.

Thus $a'=a$.  If $R<c$, more than $K\ge5(S_0+1)$ colours are unused.  C6 with $j=S_0+1$ supplies $S_0+2$ undamaged identity edges and an $(S_0+1)$-edge rainbow matching missing two vertices. Join these by fresh C4 using good colours.  Replace all $S_0+2+r-1$ identity edges by the $r$ corresponding $E_c$ blocks, and replace their $F_c$ blocks by both new rainbow matchings. The identity size decreases by one and rainbow size increases by
one: $(a,R)\mapsto(a,R+1)$.  Newly lost original edges number at most $S_0+r+1<H_0$ and $S_0+r<H_0$, respectively, again improving the objective.  Hence $R=c$.

Choose the tiny supports so $u,v=o(g)$ and then their finite configuration coefficients.  The damage and forbidden populations
in either improvement are $O_{r,K}(a+c)$, below these coefficients when $a,c\le{}m^{4/5}$.  This also makes all packing and padding choices available.  The basic outputs cover every vertex, use $v+c$ rainbow colours, and leave exactly $K$ colours unused.

\paragraph{Real contracted states.}
Let $Z$ be the matched heads of $c_0$, $m'=|Z|\ge(1-\varepsilon)m$, and put $q_c(z)=I_cJz$ whenever this is a real state in $Z$. The zero--one sum $Q_0$ has degrees at least $D-2\varepsilon{}m$ and each colour has at least $m-3\varepsilon{}m$ state edges. It is a row/column permutation of a restriction of $A$, so \refstepcounter{equation}\label{rsg:state-gap}$\|Q_0-(D/m')J_{m'}\|\le(\rho+\varepsilon)D$ \textup{(\ref{rsg:state-gap})}. Completing these partial state matchings changes the norm by at most $2\varepsilon{}m$, leaving a bound $\rho_1D$.  Parallel artificial entries are harmless even if $D>m'$.  Two real end factors and a completed middle of length $K-2$, with $K$ sufficiently large, give a fixed positive real closing probability for a $(K-1)$-letter growth word from every permitted state.  Artificial steps cost $O_K(\varepsilon)$, impermissible states cost $O_{p_0,K}(\eta_1)$, and physical/colour collisions cost $O_K(1/D)$ as in C4, including equality of the closing and final growth colours.

\paragraph{Rooted backups and the joint atom.}
Write $\Lambda(z)$ for the physical identity pair at state $z$. Each physical label occurs in at most two such pairs, and a pair
intersects at most three pairs, including itself.  For each $K$-palette $B$ choose $b_1,b_2\in{}B$ so that $|\{z:\Lambda(q_{b_2}q_{b_1}z)\cap\Lambda(z)\ne\varnothing\}|\le3m/(K-1)$. Fix $b_1$ and average over $b_2$: each start permits at most three target states and properness at most one colour per target.
Complete the order of $B$ and, from the prescribed head $y$, use $f_1,b_1,b_2,f_2,b_3,f_3,b_4,\ldots,f_{K-1},b_K$ \textup{(RP.2)}. For independent uniform fillers the $2K$ identity states are physically disjoint and the $2K-1$ letters distinct except with probability at most $\frac{3m}{D(K-1)}+\frac{K\varepsilon_*m}{D}+O_{p_0,K}(\varepsilon+\eta_1)+O_{K,\varepsilon_*}(1/D)$ \textup{(RP.3)}. Indeed every positive real position has subprobability atom $1/D$. This bounds undefined and impermissible steps and the two-step
return set.  Adjacent prescribed pairs have at most $\varepsilon_*m$ aliasing starts by $B\cap{}E_J=\varnothing$. For other collisions, every new position in a filler block is a
partial injection of the first new state, so each old physical
label excludes at most two fillers.  Repeated or exceptional filler
colours cost $O_{K,\varepsilon_*}(1/D)$.  The root pair itself is allowed even if impermissible; its future repeats are excluded.
The resulting rainbow matching misses exactly $y$ and the final state's source.

Sequentially build $L_0=\lfloor\zeta{}m\rfloor$ physically and palette-disjoint real base cycles of length $K$, avoiding the root, fixed forbidden resources and impermissible pairs.  Choose the
initial state uniformly from those still available, then uniform
growth letters conditioned on valid closure.  For small fixed $\zeta$, there are at least $m/2$ initial choices and the conditioning probability is at least a fixed $c_*>0$.  Thus, conditional on the previous cycles, $\Pr(F_i=F)\le{}CD^{-(K-1)},\Pr(a_i=a,F_i=F)\le{}C/(mD^{K-1})$ \textup{(RP.4)}. These constants can be chosen without circular dependence on $K$. First take $K$ beyond the mixing threshold, with $\rho_1^{K-2}\le{}p_0/8$ and $K-1>4800/p_0^2$. The real-end count gives closing probability at least $p_0/2$ before rejections, for sufficiently small $\varepsilon$. Then choose $\zeta,\eta_1,\varepsilon$ so that the total $O_{p_0,K}(\varepsilon+\eta_1+\zeta)+o(1)$ rejection loss leaves $c_*\ge{}p_0/8$. Thus (RP.4) holds with $C=16/p_0$, independently of $K$. Its multiple of the first term in (RP.3) is below $1/100$. Choose $\varepsilon_*$ small after $K$, and reduce $\eta_1,\varepsilon$ further, so $C$ times all of (RP.3) is below $1/20$. The atom estimates in (RP.3) concern the original unconditioned real-prefix subprobability; only afterwards do we
use the density bound (RP.4).  For fixed $F$, each base position is a partial injective function of $a$, whereas its backup positions are determined by $F,B,y$.  A specified physical collision pins at most one $a$; the joint atom makes intersection with its own backup $O_K(1/m)$, without an independence assertion.

For each $B$, conditional good-index probability is at least $9/10$. Martingale concentration and a union bound over $D^K$ palettes give linearly many good indices simultaneously; at most $K$ base palettes meet $B$.  A fixed nonroot physical label lies in a backup with conditional probability $O_K(1/m)$.  Its load $S$ obeys $\Pr(S\ge{}t)\le\binom{L_0}{t}(C'/m)^t$; take $t=C_K\log{}m$ and union bound over labels and $B$.  Fix a base family satisfying both conclusions.  Earlier basic-module colours are forbidden for
stored and free supplementary colours, but may be prescribed inputs $B$; this distinction allows the basic module's unused colours.

\paragraph{Joint support and sparse selection.}
Assign each physical label independently a source/head/neither role
with probabilities $s,s,1-2s$, force the two root labels, and sample free colours at rate $q$, where $s,q$ are inverse powers of $L$. A good base--backup union has $6K-2$ distinct nonroot labels and $K$ free base colours.  For each $B$, its logarithmic loads permit $cm/\log{}m$ disjoint unions.  Their independent survival probability is $\vartheta=s^{6K-2}q^K$, so every $B$ has at least $E_{\min}=c'\vartheta{}m/\log{}m$ eligible indices simultaneously. This is joint survival, not conditional independence after base survival.

Fix such a sample and let $\mathcal{A}$ comprise all supported bases. For an eligible $i$ let $D_i(B)$ be the other supported bases meeting its backup.  Then $|D_i(B)|\le4K-2$ and every base lies in $O_K(\log{}m)$ such sets.  Pack pairwise disjoint selector-variable sets $\{i\}\cup{}D_i(B)$, obtaining $Q_{\min}=m/L^{O_K(1)}$ trials.  Select each index of $\mathcal{A}$ independently with probability $p=8\sqrt{m}/Q_{\min}$.  Each packed trial succeeds with probability $p(1-p)^{|D_i(B)|}\ge{}p/2$, independently.  Chernoff and the polynomial union bound leave a selected eligible backup avoiding
\emph{all} selected bases for every $B$, while only $\sqrt{m}L^{O_K(1)}$ bases are selected.

Store their rainbow matchings, of total size $v'$, and every supported permitted identity edge outside those bases, plus the root edge.
Categorical roles make these edges physically disjoint.  Their number
is $\Theta(s^2m)$: the physical conflict graph on identity edges has maximum degree two, so three physically disjoint families permit
Chernoff bounds; removing selected bases is negligible.  Write this
number $u'+K$.  The successful backup has all its $2K$ identity edges in this matching.  Replace them by its base's $K$ identity edges, and replace that base's $K$ rainbow edges by the backup's $2K-1$ rainbow edges.  Outputs have sizes $u'$ and $v'+K-1$, miss $y$ and one source, and use all input colours and every stored colour except the base's closing colour.  The root edge is always
removed, so every output identity edge is permitted.

Choose $s$ with $s^2m=o(g)$; then $u',v'=o(g)$ and $u'\ge0$. Combine with the disjoint basic module and pad by $g-u-u'\hbox{identity edges},g-v-v'-K+1\hbox{rainbow edges}$. The basic module's $K$ unused colours feed the supplementary query. Direct summation gives side size $2g+1$, palette size $g+1$ and outputs $g+a-c,g+c$.  For the spare module, pad the basic module by $g-u$ identity and $g-v$ rainbow edges; its sizes are $2g,g+K$. All padding counts are nonnegative, use remaining permitted pairs
and real linear colour supplies, and avoid earlier resources.
Discard all unselected provisional support labels.

\subsection{The protected matching ledger}
\label{sg:sec:protected}\label{sg:lem:entropyinput}
\label{thm:matchinginterface}

Build the ordinary module first, followed by any fixed number of
spare modules.  At stage $i$, let $P_{<i}$ be the earlier stored palettes.  Choose candidate $c_i\notin{}P_{<i}$ and construct addition avoiding $P_{<i}\cup{}E_i\cup\{c_i\}$, where $E_i=E_{I_{c_i}}$. For the prescribed $y$, at least $D-\varepsilon{}m$ colours have real predecessors; candidate failures exclude at most $\eta_1D$, and forbidden predecessors or colours exclude only $o(m)$ more. Thus its identity choice exists without requiring the root edge
to be permitted.

Let $E_1^{(i)}$ contain all permitted identity edges with endpoints in the stored addition set, excluding an impermissible root edge.
It is admissible, contains every possible identity output, and $g\le|E_1^{(i)}|\le2g+1\le3g$; the lower bound follows from zero input.  Apply Theorem~\ref{rpe:theorem} with terminal set $E_1^{(i)}$, forbidding all earlier resources, every addition vertex and colour, and temporarily $E_i\cup\{c_i\}$.  The forbidden population is $O(b)=o(\beta{}m)$ and the resulting palette has $O(gL^{80})=o(b)$ colours.  Root endpoints may be forbidden because only the absorber interior avoids them.  Pad to exactly $b$ colours, first adding disjoint real edges in each missing colour of $(E_i\setminus{}P_{<i})\cup\{c_i\}$ \textup{(P.1)}. Every colour has a linear physical supply; temporary exceptional
exclusions are now released, while addition and earlier palettes
remain forbidden.  The bank has $b-g$ vertices per side and uses its entire fixed $b$-palette when absorbing any $g$ terminal edges.
Consequently, $B_0=b+g+1$ is the ordinary side and palette size; $B_1=b+g$ is the spare side size, with palette size $B_1+K$ \textup{(P.2)}.

For an actual balanced target of side size $z$ and ordinary palette size $z$, deleting the stored addition and bank leaves three parts of size $t=z-b-g-1$.  A residual matching of size $t-c$ leaves input $(c,c,c)$; addition supplies $g$ identity and $g+c$ rainbow edges and the bank replaces the former by $b$ rainbow edges. The total is $(t-c)+(g+c)+b=z-1$, with prescribed missing head $y$. For a spare target with $z+K$ colours, all residual parts instead have size $t=z-b-g$ and the same calculation gives $z$ edges. Precisely $K$ colours are unused, possibly including external input colours.  All bank colours are used, and the three final rainbow
pieces have disjoint palettes and disjoint vertices except for the
identity endpoints intentionally covered by the bank.

Finally $E_i\subseteq{}P_{<i}\cup{}C_{\mathrm{abs},i}$ and $c_i$ lies in its own bank.  Protected palettes remain assigned to their
interfaces.  Only spare unused colours return to the ordinary
interface; none lies in any bank.  The ordinary bank, built first,
contains its whole exceptional set.  Hence every residual or returned
input palette avoids its interface's exceptional and identity colours.
This is a rule fixed before the actual inputs exist.

\subsection{Positive capacities, adaptive extensions and the Gram gap}
\label{sec:fractional}\label{sec:sample}\label{sg:sec:gram}
\label{sg:lem:fractionalinput}

For a balanced linear three-partite hypergraph of part size $h$, let $B$ be its vertex--edge incidence matrix and $\mathcal{K}=\{z:z|_{P_i}=a_i,\sum_ia_i=0\}$. Always $\mathcal{K}\subseteq\ker(BB^{\mathsf{T}})$.

\begin{lemma}[Prescribed capacities]\label{lem:capacities}
Suppose the average degree is $d\ge\sqrt{h}$, degrees are $(1\pm{}e)d$, all six distinct-root shadow common-neighbour counts are at most $K_{\rm{}sh}d^2/h$, and $BB^{\mathsf{T}}$ has gap $\theta{}d$ on $\mathcal{K}^\perp$, with $K_{\rm{}sh}\ge1$, $0<\theta\le1$.  Capacities with equal part sums and $|b_v-1|\le\lambda$ have realizing weights in $[1/(2d),3/(2d)]$ whenever $e+\lambda\le\theta/(120\sqrt{K_{\rm{}sh}})$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $\delta=e+\lambda$, $f_v=db_v-d_v$, and solve $BB^{\mathsf{T}}z=f$ in $\mathcal{K}^\perp$.  Equal part sums give $f\perp\mathcal{K}$, and $\|z\|_2\le\delta\sqrt{3h}/\theta$. For $T=\operatorname{diag}(d_v)$, $P=T^{-1}(BB^{\mathsf{T}}-T)$ and $a=T^{-1}f$, every row of $P$ sums to two and $z=a-Pa+P^2z$, with $\|a-Pa\|_\infty\le6\delta$. If $x$ is a row of a normalized shadow, then $\|x\|_1=1$ and $\|x\|_2^2\le1/d_{\min}$.  For a following normalized shadow $Q$, its distinct-row inner products are at most $K_{\rm{}sh}d^2/(hd_{\min}^2)$ and diagonal ones at most $1/d_{\min}$.  Therefore $\|xQ\|_2^2\le{}d_{\min}^{-2}+K_{\rm{}sh}d^2/(hd_{\min}^2)\le\frac{1+K_{\rm{}sh}}{(1-e)^2h}\le4K_{\rm{}sh}/h$. Here $d^2\ge{}h$, $K_{\rm{}sh}\ge1$, and the hypothesis gives $e\le1/120$, so the last inequality holds. Each of the four two-step contributions has norm at most $2\sqrt{K_{\rm{}sh}/h}$. Their sum gives each row of $P^2$ norm at most $8\sqrt{K_{\rm{}sh}/h}$.  Thus $\|z\|_\infty\le20\sqrt{K_{\rm{}sh}}\delta/\theta$. Set $w_e=(1+\sum_{v\in{}e}z_v)/d$.  Weighted degrees are exactly $(d_v+(BB^{\mathsf{T}}z)_v)/d=b_v$, and the three corrections sum in absolute value to at most $1/2$, proving the bounds.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Small arbitrary extensions]\label{lem:extension}
Suppose the preceding core has $e\le\theta/(240\sqrt{K_{\rm{}sh}})$. Assume every possible ambient root has $(1\pm{}e)d$ link edges into the other two core parts, with the same anchored six upper-shadow
bounds for every pair of ambient roots in each role.  For sufficiently
small fixed $\beta_0=\beta_0(\theta,K_{\rm{}sh})>0$, every balanced extension by $t\le\beta_0h$ vertices per part has a fractional perfect matching with weights at most $2/d$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $u=\sqrt{\beta_0}$.  For each added type $i$, use edges with one added coordinate and two core coordinates.  Delete those meeting
a core vertex incident to more than $ud$ edges of that type. An added root $x$ loses, in one overloaded part, at most $(ud)^{-1}\sum_{x'\text{ added in type }i}|N(x)\cap{}N(x')|\le(1+e)/u+K_{\rm{}sh}ud$. The term $x'=x$ is a degree; others use the anchored bound. For small $u$ and large $h$, at least $(1-e-2K_{\rm{}sh}u)d-2(1+e)/u\ge{}d/2$ edges remain at each root. Weight them equally to give it degree one.  A core vertex receives
at most $2u$ from each other added type, hence at most $4u$ total. Each core part receives total load exactly $2t$, so the remaining capacities have common sum $h-2t$ and lie in $[1-4u,1]$. Choose $4u\le\theta/(240\sqrt{K_{\rm{}sh}})$ and $2K_{\rm{}sh}u\le1/8$; Lemma~\ref{lem:capacities} fills them. Edges with two or three added coordinates receive weight zero.
This construction works for every target deterministically.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Rounding]\label{cor:rounding}
If $d=\Theta(h)$, these cores and extensions have matchings missing $O(h^{2/3}\log^4h)$ vertices per part.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Retain edge $e$ independently with probability $dw_e/3\le2/3$. Every expected degree is $d/3$; Chernoff gives an outcome with degrees $d/3\pm{}d^{2/3}$. Let $N$ be the part size of the actual target; here $N=\Theta(h)$ and $d=\Theta(h)$. The retained hypergraph is still linear and three-uniform. The edge-colouring
theorem of Molloy--Reed~\cite{MolloyReed} gives $\chi'\le\Delta+C\Delta^{2/3}(\log\Delta)^4,\Delta\le{}d/3+d^{2/3}$, where $C$ is absolute for uniformity three. It has at least $N(d/3-d^{2/3})$ edges, so a largest colour class is a matching of size $\frac{N(d/3-d^{2/3})}{d/3+d^{2/3}+C(d/3+d^{2/3})^{2/3}(\log(d/3+d^{2/3}))^4}\ge{}N-O(h^{2/3}\log^4h)$. Equal part sizes make the three deficits equal. Each actual target
uses its own thinning; simultaneous deterministic existence does not
require one random thinning for all targets.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The sampled certificate.}
\label{lem:shadows}\label{lem:gram}\label{sg:lem:gram}
In Proposition~\ref{rsg:proposition} put $s_X=v_0/|X|$, $s_Y=v_0/|Y|$, $q=v_0/D$ and $d_0=v_0^2/m$.  Choose $\kappa$ so $s_X+s_Y<1$. Anchored source, target and colour degrees have expectations $qs_Yd_H(x)$, $qs_Xd_H(y)$ and $s_Xs_Y|M_c|$, respectively, all $(1+O_{p_0}(\varepsilon))d_0$.  An anchored root is forced into its required role; incompatible internal appearances are removed,
changing expectations only by $O(1)$.  Properness and injectivity make the effect of each trial on every anchored statistic bounded.
Bounded differences with error $m^{4/5}$ and a polynomial union bound therefore give uniform degree and shadow estimates.

Two distinct vertex roots have $D-O(\varepsilon{}m)$ common defined colours, and two colours have $m-O(\varepsilon{}m)$ common real domains.  Their relevant image labels are distinct by properness
and looplessness.  The two vertex--colour shadows consequently
have distinct-root codegrees $(1+O_{p_0}(\varepsilon))d_0^2/v_0+o(m)$ in both directions. For all six upper shadows, their expectation ratios to $d_0^2/v_0=\kappa^3m$ are bounded respectively by $\frac{m^2}{|Y|D},\frac{m^2}{|X|D},(m/|Y|)^2,(m/|Y|)^2,(m/|X|)^2,(m/|X|)^2$. The first two use at most $D$ common graph neighbours; the other four use the two vertex--colour shadows.  Thus $K_{\rm{}sh}=10p_0^{-2}$ suffices, independently of $\kappa$. Centre a vertex--colour shadow by $(d_0/v_0)J$.  Expanding its product with its transpose and using the degree and codegree bounds
gives maximum absolute row sum $O_{p_0}(\varepsilon)d_0^2+o(m^2)$; its norm is $O_{p_0}(\sqrt\varepsilon)d_0+o(m)$.

For the source--target shadow let $E=A-(D/m)J_m$.  Split off singular values at least $\delta{}m$.  There are $O_\delta(1)$ because $\|E\|_F^2=O(m^2)$, and their singular vectors have coordinates $O_\delta(m^{-1/2})$, using the row and column norm bounds.  The sampled left and right singular-vector Grams concentrate
separately below $s_XI+o(1)$ and $s_YI+o(1)$; sampling only real copies gives these inequalities even when dummy coordinates exist.
Their physical coupling does not prevent intersecting the two
events.  Restriction of the remaining operator has norm at most $\delta{}m$.  Sending $\delta$ to zero gives $\|E[X_0,Y_0]\|\le\sqrt{s_Xs_Y}\rho{}D+o(m)$. Conditional on physical roles, independent colour sampling is a sum
of partial permutation matrices of norm at most one, so matrix
concentration yields \refstepcounter{equation}\label{rsg:shadow-gap}$\left\|T-\frac{d_0}{v_0}J\right\|\le{}q\sqrt{s_Xs_Y}\rho{}D+o(m)\le(\rho+O(\varepsilon))d_0+o(m)$ \textup{(\ref{rsg:shadow-gap})}. Here the mean coefficient is exactly $qD/m=d_0/v_0$.

The incidence Gram model has diagonal blocks $d_0I$, off-diagonal blocks $(d_0/v_0)J$, eigenvalue zero on $\mathcal{K}$, $3d_0$ on global constants and $d_0$ on the remaining space.  Its only large centred shadow and its transpose together have norm at most $(\rho+O(\varepsilon))d_0+o(m)$.  Thus the true gap is at least $(1-\rho-O_{p_0}(\sqrt\varepsilon))d_0-o(m)$. Balancing or changing $z=o(m)$ resources changes padded shadow norms by $O(\sqrt{mz})=o(m)$ and anchored counts by $o(m)$. Against the actual average degree $d=(1+O_{p_0}(\varepsilon))d_0+o(m)$, the relative degree error is $O_{p_0}(\varepsilon)+o(1)$. Take $\varepsilon$ below the capacity threshold and $\theta=(1-\rho)/4$.  One may choose $\sqrt{\beta_0}\le\min\{\theta/(960\sqrt{K_{\rm{}sh}}),1/(16K_{\rm{}sh})\}$. This proves (iv), with tolerance independent of $\kappa$. For targets omitting a small part of their core, intersect and trim
the three cores equally, then trim targets equally while retaining
that core.  Provided these deletions are below the fixed shadow and
gap margins, and the relative extension is below $\beta_0$, the same deterministic lemmas apply.  No union bound over adaptive
targets is needed.  This completes Proposition~\ref{rsg:proposition}.

\subsection{The above-half consequence}
\label{pf:section}

\begin{theorem}[Robust local path with prescribed start]
\label{pf:theorem}
For every $\gamma>0$ there is $\varepsilon_0>0$ such that, for large $m$, a loopless simple properly incoming/outgoing coloured digraph on $m$ vertices with exactly $D$ colours, where $(1/2+\gamma)m\le{}D\le{}m$, degrees in $[D-\varepsilon_0m,D]$ and colour sizes at least $(1-\varepsilon_0)m$, has a rainbow directed path of $D-1$ arcs from every prescribed vertex $y$.  It may also avoid $F\subseteq{}V\setminus\{y\}$ with $|F|\le\varepsilon_0m$, provided $D\le{}m-|F|$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Complete the colour matchings, writing $\widetilde{A}=A+B$. Here every row and column of $B$ has sum at most $\varepsilon{}m$, so $\|B\|\le\varepsilon{}m$.  For $f\perp\mathbf1$, $Af=-(J-A)f$, with $\|J-A\|\le{}m-D+\varepsilon{}m$. Regularity of $\widetilde{A}$ therefore gives $\|\widetilde{A}-(D/m)J\|\le{}m-D+2\varepsilon{}m,\|A-(D/m)J\|\le{}m-D+3\varepsilon{}m$. For small $\varepsilon$ this is a fixed ratio below $D$. Apply Proposition~\ref{rsg:proposition}, choosing its errors after
the finitely many sampling rates in Lemma~\ref{rou:oneblock}.
Its rooted ordinary module, spare modules and actual-core certificates
meet that lemma's hypotheses with ambient size $m$ and path capacity $D$.  The lemma also allows the zero outside quota when $m=D$ and gives the required real path from $y$.  The artificial completions were only counting devices and supply none of its arcs.

For the forbidden version first prove the assertion with error
allowance $4\varepsilon_0$ and apply it to $H-F$, of order $m'=m-|F|$.  A remaining vertex loses at most $|F|$ neighbours in either direction; a colour loses at most $2|F|$ arcs.  Relative to $D$ and $m'$, deficits are at most $\varepsilon_0m+|F|\le4\varepsilon_0m'$ for small $\varepsilon_0$. The density lower bound persists, and $D\le{}m'$ is the stipulated physical capacity.  The resulting path avoids $F$.
\end{proof}
