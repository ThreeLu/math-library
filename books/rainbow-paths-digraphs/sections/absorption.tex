\section{Partial-factorization absorption}
\label{rpe:section}\label{ent:section}
Let $\mathcal{H}$ be a simple properly $D$-coloured bipartite graph on $X\cup{}Y$, with $m=\max(|X|,|Y|)$ and $v=|X|+|Y|$. Each side is injectively labelled by physical vertices; equal-labelled
opposite copies are nonadjacent. A configuration is \emph{admissible}
when it uses each physical label at most once. All vertex avoidance
below forbids both copies of a physical vertex. Assume \refstepcounter{equation}\label{rpe:model}$D\ge{}p_0m,(1-\delta)D\le{}d(x)\le{}D(x\in{}X\cup{}Y)$ \textup{(\ref{rpe:model})}. Counting edges gives \refstepcounter{equation}\label{rpe:rectangle}$\min(|X|,|Y|)\ge(1-\delta)m,p:=D/v\ge{}p_*:=p_0/2$ \textup{(\ref{rpe:rectangle})}.

Put $L=\log{}v$ and $\beta=L^{-12000}$. A local absorber for $E$ consists of an admissible interior $W$ and a palette $C$, avoiding all root vertices and the root colour $c$ respectively, such that $|W\cap{}X|=|W\cap{}Y|=|C|-1$, $|C|\le{}L^{80}$, and $W\cup{}V(e)$ has an exactly-$C$ rainbow perfect matching for every $e\in{}E$. Its interior and palette may avoid any physical and colour forbidden sets of size at most $\beta{}v/100$ each; named roots are exempt from interior avoidance.

For $g\to\infty$, $gL^{80}=o(b)$ and $b=o(\beta{}m)$, a $g$-absorber for $E_1$ instead has $|C|=O(gL^{80})$, $|W\cap{}X|=|W\cap{}Y|=|C|-g$, and matches $W\cup{}V(E')$ for every $g$-subset $E'\subseteq{}E_1$. Its forbidden budgets are $\beta{}m/100$ each. If every prescribed padding colour has $|M_a|\ge\kappa_0m$ for fixed $\kappa_0>0$ \refstepcounter{equation}\label{rpe:padding-colours}\textup{(\ref{rpe:padding-colours})}, any prescribed $o(b)$-palette avoiding the forbidden palette can be included, with final sizes $|C|=b$ and $|W\cap{}X|=|W\cap{}Y|=b-g$. Padding may include $c$.

\begin{theorem}[Partial-factorization absorption]\label{rpe:theorem}\label{ent:local-absorber}
For $0<\eta<1/100$, sufficiently small $\delta$ and large $m$, there are at least $(1-\eta)D$ candidate colours with designated pools $|E_c^0|\ge(1-\eta)m$. Every admissible $E\subseteq{}E_c^0$, $1\le|E|\le100$, has a local absorber. Under the stated scale assumptions, every admissible $E_1\subseteq{}E_c^0$, $g\le|E_1|\le3g$, has a $g$-absorber. Both satisfy the avoidance budgets above, and the latter admits the prescribed padding. The pools are fixed before all queries.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Entropy, decorated witnesses, and loads}
\label{ent:entropy-subsection}
Write $C_a$ for the symmetric partial-matching matrix, $D_a=C_a^2$, and $A=\sum_aC_a$. On $\Omega=X^{(2)}\cup{}Y^{(2)}$, move $(x,y)$ simultaneously to $(I_ax,I_ay)$ if both edges exist; otherwise hold both coordinates. The resulting map $\tau_a$ is an involution. On $\widehat\Omega=\Omega\times\{0,1\}$ let $T_a$ also flip the last coordinate precisely when an actual move occurs.
The symmetric stochastic chain $\mathsf{L}=I/2+\sum_aT_a/(2D)$ has uniform stationary measure, also when the sides are unequal.
The invalid-colour fraction satisfies \refstepcounter{equation}\label{rpe:invalid}$b(z)\le2\delta$ \textup{(\ref{rpe:invalid})}. An arm is a sequence of $t$ independent records, taking $0$ with probability $1/2$ and each colour with probability $1/(2D)$. Invalid attempted colours remain decorations, not output edges.

Write $\mu_z=\mathsf{L}^t((z,0),\cdot)$ and let $Q_z^i$ be twice its phase-$i$ endpoint matrix. Put $u_z=D^{-1}\sum_a\operatorname{TV}(\mu_z,(T_a)_*\mu_z)$, $q_{a,z}=\operatorname{TV}(\mu_z,\mathsf{L}^t(T_a(z,0),\cdot))$, and $\mathcal{K}(B,C)=\langle{}B,ACA\rangle$. The latter is four times the probability that independent arms from $z,I_az$ have even actual lengths and both closing edges. Expectations below use uniform roots and, for $q_{a,z}$, a uniform colour.

\begin{lemma}[Entropy estimate]\label{rpe:analytic}
For integer $t\ge{}C\log{}v$, each $Q_z^i$ has mass $1\pm2\delta$ and row and column sums at most $4/D$. Both $\mathbb{E}u_z^2$ and $\mathbb{E}q_{a,z}^2$ are at most $2\log(2)\Delta_t$, where $0\le\Delta_t\le4\log{}v/t$ \refstepcounter{equation}\label{rpe:entropy-bounds}\textup{(\ref{rpe:entropy-bounds})}. For every valid root colour $a$, $\mathcal{K}(Q_z^0,Q_{I_az}^0)\ge{}p^2-8u_z-14\delta-6q_{a,z}$ \refstepcounter{equation}\label{rpe:polarization}\textup{(\ref{rpe:polarization})}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a stationary chain put $\mathsf{H}_t=H(Z_t\mid{}Z_0)$, in bits. The Markov property gives $\Delta_t=\mathsf{H}_{t+1}-\mathsf{H}_t=I(Z_1;Z_{t+1}\mid{}Z_0)$; conditional data processing makes these increments nonincreasing.
Since $\mathsf{H}_t\le\log_2(2|\Omega|)$, the asserted bound follows. The last- and first-step mixtures are, respectively, $\mu_z^{t+1}=\mu_z^t/2+\sum_a(T_a)_*\mu_z^t/(2D)$ and $\mu_z^{t+1}=\mu_z^t/2+\sum_a\mu_{T_az}^t/(2D)$. Their average Jensen--Shannon divergences both equal $\Delta_t$. For either mixture $M=P_0/2+\sum_aP_a/(2D)$, triangle inequality and Pinsker give $D^{-1}\sum_a\operatorname{TV}(P_0,P_a)^2\le2\log(2)\operatorname{JS}$. Jensen and the commuting phase flip prove the two mean-square bounds.
No commutation of different colour maps is required.

If $M_s$ is a coordinate's largest point probability, properness gives $M_{s+1}\le(1/2+\delta)M_s+1/(2D)$, hence $M_t\le2/D$. For $\chi(z,i)=(-1)^i$, $\mathsf{L}\chi=b(z)\chi$ and $\|\mathsf{L}^t\chi\|_\infty\le2\delta$, proving the phase masses. The phase-zero slice after $T_a$ is $C_aQ_z^1C_a+Q_z^0\odot{}B_a$, where $B_a$ indicates invalid pairs. Consequently $\|Q_z^0-C_aQ_z^1C_a\|_1\le4\operatorname{TV}(\mu_z,(T_a)_*\mu_z)+\|Q_z^0\odot{}B_a\|_1$. Summing the invalid mass and using $AQ_z^1-Q_z^0A=\sum_a(C_aQ_z^1C_a-Q_z^0)C_a+\sum_aC_aQ_z^1(I-D_a)$ yields $\|AQ_z^1-Q_z^0A\|_1\le4Du_z+4\delta{}D$. Each entry is at most $4/D$, so the squared Frobenius difference is at most $16u_z+16\delta$. Both entry sums are at least $(1-3\delta)D$, hence their squared norms are at least $(1-3\delta)^2p^2$. Polarization gives $\mathcal{K}(Q_z^0,Q_z^1)\ge{}p^2-8u_z-14\delta$. For a valid root, phase commutation gives $\|Q_z^1-Q_{I_az}^0\|_1\le4q_{a,z}$; the zero--one closing kernel and mass at most $1+2\delta$ give the final $6q_{a,z}$ loss.
\end{proof}

Choose a small fixed $\epsilon=\epsilon(p_0,\eta)$, then $C\ge10^8p_*^{-4}\epsilon^{-1}$, $t=\lceil{}C\log{}v\rceil$, $H=2t+2$, and $\sigma>0$ such that \refstepcounter{equation}\label{rpe:mask-hierarchy}$4C\mathfrak{h}(\sigma)<1/4,\mathfrak{h}(x)=-x\log{}x-(1-x)\log(1-x)$ \textup{(\ref{rpe:mask-hierarchy})}. Finally take $\delta\ll{}p_*,\epsilon,\sigma$. A retained witness records the original directed roots $(c,z)$ and both complete attempted arms $\rho,\lambda$, starting at $I_cz,z$. Both actual lengths are positive even numbers at least two; its two even cycles are physically disjoint and admissible;
all nonzero attempts and $c$ are distinct; the closing colours avoid all of these; and at most $2\sigma{}t$ attempts are invalid. The closing colours may equal one another.

\begin{lemma}[Rich pairs]\label{rpe:rich}
All but a $2\epsilon$ fraction of $(c,z)\in\mathcal{D}\times\Omega$ have valid admissible roots and retained-witness probability at least $\kappa=p_*^2/16$. Deficiency is invariant under $z\mapsto{}I_cz$ on valid roots.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Markov applied to the entropy bounds excludes $O(p_*^{-4}/C)$ root pairs; outside them, the unfiltered even closing probability is at least $p_*^2/8$. Invalid roots cost $2\delta$, and physical root overlaps cost $O(1/m)$. Actual moves dominate $\operatorname{Bin}(t,(1-2\delta)/2)$, so lengths below two cost $o(1)$. Invalid attempts are dominated by $\operatorname{Bin}(2t,\delta)$; their tail above $2\sigma{}t$ is at most $(e\delta/\sigma)^{2\sigma{}t}=o(1)$. Attempt repetitions cost $O(t^2/D)$.

For vertex collisions expose each deterministic time at which a later
actual move hits a previous physical label: at most two colours work,
with probability at most $1/D$. Sum over time pairs. Equal-time coordinates are distinct same-side copies. Between arms fix one
complete arm first; the other remains independent. Zero-time positions
are the prescribed root blocks. The resulting cost is $O(t^2/D)$. For a closing colour equal to a particular attempted letter, fix the
whole arm containing that attempt, including held and future attempts.
Its endpoint and the forbidden letter are now fixed. In the other
independent arm enumerate deterministic times $j$ for its last actual move. On the bad event, that move must reach the fixed neighbour of
the first endpoint in the forbidden colour. Given the prefix, at most
one letter does this, costing $1/(2D)$. Drop the restriction that all later attempts hold and sum their probabilities to one. Summing
over $j$, the two closing edges and all attempts costs $O(t^2/D)$. For $c$ fix either arm and use the same argument. Thus no conditioning on a random last move, or on its being valid, occurs. All losses are
unconditional and eventually below $p_*^2/16$. Interchanging the arms proves the asserted symmetry, preserving record probabilities and masks.
\end{proof}

For a record with $k$ nonzero attempts write $\Pr(\rho)=2^{-t}D^{-k}$. Give each retained original witness weight \refstepcounter{equation}\label{rpe:weight}$\omega=\frac{D^2}{P(H)}\Pr(\rho)\Pr(\lambda),P(H)=10^{12}(H+1)^8$ \textup{(\ref{rpe:weight})}. A use at another common-edge pair retains this entire original label
and weight; it is not resampled. Every weight is less than one.

The five queries fix, in order: (1) an exchange colour and a different common colour; (2) an exchange colour and a physical vertex; (3) a corresponding common-edge pair and another colour; (4) such a pair and a physical vertex outside its root blocks; (5) one incidence edge. Queries (3)--(4) pool all common roles.

\begin{lemma}[Five loads]\label{rpe:loads}\label{ent:loads}
The five query loads are bounded respectively by $v^3,v^3,v,v,v^3$. Total mass, original-colour mass and original-root-edge mass are at most $v^2D^3/P$, $v^2D^2/P$ and $4vD^2/P$. Exchange-colour incident mass is at most $50v^4$. At a fixed original colour, another physical vertex or distinct colour has load at most $v^3$. Every rich original pair has mass at least $\kappa{}D^2/P$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Two counting rules handle holds. Every fixed attempted prefix is a
permutation of the \emph{pair} space, also after the root involution.
Hence at most $v$ original pairs reach a specified coordinate, and at most one reaches a specified ordered pair. By simplicity and
properness, a fresh attempted step
hits a specified physical label with probability at most $1/D$. Reaching a prescribed copy at a last actual move costs at most $t/(2D)$: sum deterministic last-move positions, dropping future-hold conditions. A uniform root-colour step has physical point bound $2/D$. These are pair-space statements; a held one-coordinate map need not
be a permutation.

(1) If the specified common colour is the original colour, fix it;
otherwise pin its actual attempted position, costing $1/(2D)$, while summing the $D$ original colours. Fix that arm. The prescribed exchange forces the independent arm's endpoint at cost $O(t/D)$. Summing $v^2$ roots and multiplying by $D^2/P$ gives $O(H^2)v^2D/P$.
(2) Place the specified vertex in one arm, possibly at time zero.
For each fixed record that arm leaves at most $2v$ root pairs. The other arm's endpoint is forced as above, giving $O(H^2)vD^2/P$.

(3) For original common roots, both roots and their colour are fixed;
another common colour pins an attempted letter, and an exchange pins
the other arm's endpoint. The bound is $O(H^2)D/P$. For a nonoriginal common pair, specify its position and orientations.
Its letter is pinned, costing $1/(2D)$; pair invertibility uniquely recovers the original roots for fixed records and original colour.
Another common colour pins a second attempt, or fixes the original
colour and removes its factor $D$. For an exchange, anchor this arm and expose the other arm's last actual move. These give $O(H^3)D/P$.
(4) For a nonoriginal common pair, pin its time position $j$, its letter $\gamma$, and its ordered endpoint pairs. The following change of variables explains why reanchoring does not bias the other
attempts. For every fixed complete letter array, the prefix map $F$ on the pair space is a permutation, so $\sum_{z\in\Omega}\mathbf1_{\{Fz=z_*\}}=1$ for the prescribed anchor pair $z_*$. Thus summing the original roots with counting measure leaves exactly the product measure on all free letters;
it does not condition a randomly chosen root on reaching $z_*$. Restrictions such as valid original roots and admissibility can
then be discarded for an upper bound. The pinned step is actual
because its two endpoint pairs are the prescribed matching edges.

Delete the exchanges and the anchored common edge. The remaining
paired common path splits into two arms from its known endpoints.
Choose the arm and recorded position containing the prescribed
physical vertex. Reversed attempted steps use the same pair
involutions, in reverse order. Along this segment all letter indices
are distinct and free; the pinned step is not traversed. Treat the
original colour, if encountered, as an independent uniform letter,
putting back the factor $D$ for its original counting measure. Starting from the fixed endpoint, reveal these letters in traversal
order. At any deterministic time a first actual hit of the specified
label costs at most $1/D$ (at most $2/D$ for the root-colour step). The label lies outside the anchor blocks, so every retained occurrence
requires such a hit. Summing over times costs $O(H/D)$, regardless of which intervening attempts hold. The free root-colour factor $D$ cancels the pinned-attempt factor $1/(2D)$, giving $O(H^3)D/P$ after positions and orientations. For an original anchor the colour
and roots are fixed from the outset and both cancelling factors are
absent; the same first-hit bound applies. No conditional law of
effective moving letters is invoked.
(5) A common edge fixes a letter and one coordinate, giving $O(H)vD^2/P$. An exchange edge fixes one endpoint in each arm; pair invertibility handles the first and last-move exposure the second,
giving $O(H^2)vD^2/P$.

Additional role marks cost at most $H$; specifying a common pair already specifies its mark. Positions, orientations and physical copies thus
cost at most a fixed multiple of $(H+1)^4$, absorbed by $P$ since $D\le{}v$. The same counts with the original colour fixed give the two extra coordinate loads; omitting its common-colour restriction
gives exchange incident mass $O(Hv^2D^2/P)$. Complete record probabilities sum to one. There are at most $v^2D$ original roots and at most $4v$ at a specified root edge, proving the remaining mass bounds without
an extra role factor. The rich-pair assertion is immediate.
\end{proof}

The hold-mask argument uses the surviving-mass condition $\sum_{z,\rho,\lambda}\Pr(\rho)\Pr(\lambda)\mathbf1_{\{(c,z,\rho,\lambda)\text{ retained}\}}\ge\kappa'v^2$ for fixed $\kappa'>0$ \refstepcounter{equation}\label{rpe:surviving-mass}\textup{(\ref{rpe:surviving-mass})}.

\begin{lemma}[Hold-mask extraction]\label{rpe:fan}
For fixed $c$, any preliminarily filtered family satisfying \eqref{rpe:surviving-mass} contains a fixed first cycle and actual common word with $\Omega_{\kappa'}(v^{3/4}/H^2)$ mutually physically disjoint second cycles avoiding the first.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Averaging fixes records with $\kappa'v^2$ roots and then a first root with $\kappa'v$ partners. Their invalid-position masks have at most $\sum_{j\le2\sigma{}t}\binom{2t}{j}\le\exp(2t\mathfrak{h}(\sigma)+O(\log{}t))\le{}v^{1/4}$ possibilities. Fix one mask. The actual words and first cycle are now fixed; they
were not determined by the attempted records alone. A fixed actual
word is a partial bijection. A physical vertex occurs in at most $2H$ partner cycles, so greedy disjoint selection loses at most $2H^2$ partners per choice. All preliminary filters survive.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Colour classes and cleaning}
\label{ent:classes-subsection}
A colour-switcher consists of two rainbow matchings on the same
admissible set, whose palettes differ only in their exchange colours.
Its order is either matching size. A basic witness supplies one of
order $h\le{}H$, by taking opposite alternating matchings on its two cycles. The common palette omits the exchanges. In the union of two matching states, retain edge multiplicities; a shared edge forms an even two-cycle. Chaining switches
uses disjoint physical sets and common palettes, with joining colours
excluded from every common palette; each joining colour then occurs
once in each final alternative.

Use parameters \refstepcounter{equation}\label{ent:class-parameters}$\xi=L^{-400},K=L^{600},\eta_c=L^{-1000},\varepsilon=L^{-10000},L_0=\lceil{}L^{80}\rceil,\ell=\lceil500HL^3\rceil$ \textup{(\ref{ent:class-parameters})}. For large $v$, $H\le{}L^{34}$, $P(H)\le{}L^{140}$ and $\ell\le{}L^{38}$. A class $Q$ is exchangeable if, for initial forbidden physical and common-colour sets of size $\varepsilon{}v$ with at most $\varepsilon|Q|$ forbidden colours in $Q$, an exceptional set $B_Q\supseteq{}Q\cap\bar{C}$, $|B_Q|\le\eta_c|Q|$, can be chosen
\emph{before} the two exchange endpoints and later forbidden sets.
Any two distinct colours outside $B_Q$ then have a switcher of order at most $\ell$ with initial avoidance and any later physical/common-colour avoidance of size $L_0$. If $Q\cap\bar{C}=\varnothing$, $B_Q$ is empty. Exchange endpoints may themselves be later forbidden common colours.

\begin{lemma}[Weighted classes]\label{ent:classes}
There are exchangeable classes such that each colour belongs to at
most $L^2/\xi$ classes larger than two. Calling colours equivalent if equal or in a common class, nonequivalent witnesses have total
original weight at most $\xi{}v^5$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We use two explicit external inputs \cite[Theorem 3.17, Lemma 3.22]{Montgomery}.
An $(\alpha,\Delta)$-expander retains external expansion $\alpha$ for sets of size at most two thirds after any maximum-degree-$\Delta$ edge deletion. With $\alpha=(16L)^{-1}$ every graph contains such a subgraph $F$ with deletion budget $\alpha\bar{d}(F)$, $\bar{d}(F)\ge\bar{d}(R)/2$, and $\delta(F)\ge\bar{d}(F)/2$. In an $(\alpha,\Delta)$-expander with minimum degree $d_*\ge4\Delta$, after allowable edge deletion and vertex deletion $V_0$, all pairs outside a set $B\supseteq{}V_0$ of size at most $10^4|V_0|\Delta(F)/(\alpha{}d_*)$ have $r_*$ internally disjoint paths of length at most $400L^3$, provided $r_*\le\alpha{}d_*/(10^6L^3)$.

Let $w_{ab}$ be exchange-pair mass. The five loads may be bounded by $20v^3$, $50v^3$, $1000v$, $600v$, and $100v^3$. If $w_{ab}\ge150\varepsilon{}v^4$, forbidden sets of size $2\varepsilon{}v$ remove at most $140\varepsilon{}v^4$, giving a two-colour class, including the later allowance.
Put $\lambda=\xi/4$, $\xi'=\xi/(4K)$. Among pairs of mass at least $\lambda{}v^3$, at most $\xi'v^2$ fail robust order-$3H$ exchangeability. Otherwise choose an inclusion-maximal subgraph $R'$ of those failing pairs together with one positive-weight witness per edge,
such that the assigned witnesses are disjoint in physical vertices
and common colours at each star. Orient each omitted edge toward
a star blocking half its mass. A degree-$a$ star uses at most $2Ha$ vertices and $Ha$ common colours; hence $d^+(c)\lambda{}v^3/2\le120Hv^3d_{R'}(c)$ and $e(R')\ge\lambda\xi'v^2/(1000H)\ge{}L^{-1436}v^2$. Cauchy--Schwarz counts at least $L^{-5745}v^4$ four-cycles
(the $O(v^3)$ degenerate walks are negligible). Some edge $cd$ has $L^{-5746}v^2$ three-edge alternatives $cc'c''d$. Initial/later forbidden resources remove $O(\varepsilon{}v+L_0)$ choices at the $c$-star, so a surviving $c'$ has $L^{-5747}v$ choices of $c''$. At the $c'$- and $d$-stars each physical vertex or common colour occurs at most twice; all crossed conflicts,
joining-colour conflicts, and conflicts with the first witness cost $O(\varepsilon{}v+H+L_0)$ further choices. Three surviving witnesses chain, contradicting failure. This proves the bound for light pairs.

Very light pairs, below $\xi{}v^3/4$, cost $\xi{}v^5/4$; uncovered pairs up to $Kv^3$ cost another $\xi{}v^5/4$. Split $(Kv^3,150\varepsilon{}v^4]$ into $r\le4L$ dyadic intervals $(w_i,2w_i]$, and put $d_i=\xi{}v^4/(8rw_i)$, $\Delta_i=(400r/\xi)d_i$. The colour graph has maximum degree at most $\Delta_i$. Take maximal edge-disjoint $(\alpha,\alpha{}d_i)$-expanders with minimum degree $d_i$. Deletion budgets are rounded down to integers; the deleted-degree
estimate below is at most $(140/320)\alpha{}d_i$, leaving the margin needed for this rounding. The other inequalities have greater slack.
The parameter inequalities are \refstepcounter{equation}\label{ent:expander-inequalities}$d_iw_i\ge320\varepsilon{}v^4/\alpha,\Delta_i/d_i\le1/\eta_c,d_i\ge10^{10}L_0L^4,w_i\ge10^8HL_0L^3v^3$ \textup{(\ref{ent:expander-inequalities})}. Indeed their respective worst powers are $L^{-401}v^4$, $O(L^{401})$, $\Omega(L^{9599})$, and $L^{600}v^3$. Delete an expander edge when half its mass hits initial resources.
Its deleted degree is at most $140\varepsilon{}v^4/w_i\le\alpha{}d_i$. The connection input with $r_*=2L_0$ gives an exceptional set of size $10^4\varepsilon|Q|/(\alpha\eta_c)\le\eta_c|Q|$. One path avoids later colours internally. Along it select witnesses
avoiding all path colours as common colours, previous resources and
later forbidden sets. Additional lost mass is $O(v^3[L_0+HL^3])<w_i/2$, so the chain has order at most $\ell$. The residual graph has average degree at most $4d_i$ by the first expander input, leaving mass at most $4d_ivw_i=\xi{}v^5/(2r)$. Each large-class membership spends $d_iw_i$ incident mass; the $50v^4$ incident bound gives at most $400r/\xi\le{}L^2/\xi$ memberships. All selections require positive weight, not integral weight.
\end{proof}

Set \refstepcounter{equation}\label{ent:cleaning-parameters}$\theta=L^{-500},q_0=L^{-250},\tau=L^{-170},\zeta=L^{-50},b_0=L^{-150}$ \textup{(\ref{ent:cleaning-parameters})}. For initial forbidden sets of size $10\beta{}v$, discard large classes whose forbidden-colour fraction exceeds $\varepsilon$. Their union has size at most $10\beta{}vL^2/(\varepsilon\xi)$; exceptions in the other classes total at most $\eta_cvL^2/\xi$. Adding the initial palette gives a collective set $B$ of size at most $\theta{}v$. Equivalent colours outside $B$ have the simultaneous initial/later avoidance above, including two-element classes.

An edge-switcher of order $s$ for disjoint roots $e,f$ has an admissible interior of size $2s-2$ and an $s$-palette perfectly matching either root with that interior. It is \emph{separated}
if its two terminal components are odd paths joining each root's
own endpoints, with even cycles elsewhere. Let $\mathcal{R}$ contain same-colour pairs with such a switcher of order at most $H+\ell$, robust against $\beta{}v$ physical vertices and output colours. Root endpoints and root colour may be forbidden: avoidance concerns
the interior and output palette.

For a common pair put $\mu(e,f)$ equal to its mass over all roles. If $\mu\ge{}q_0v^2$ but the pair is not in $\mathcal{R}$, fix failing forbidden sets. The rooted loads remove at most $(\beta+\theta)v^2<q_0v^2/4$. A surviving equivalent-exchange witness yields a switcher: with equal exchanges delete the roots
from opposite alternating matchings; otherwise add a fresh exchange
colour-switcher, physically disjoint and with common palette avoiding
the basic palette. Cross its alternatives with the two root-deleted
matchings. The common output has order $h+s-1\le{}H+\ell$. Separate even cycles lose separate root edges, leaving the required
odd terminal paths. Thus at least half of $\mu$ is nonequivalent. Delete witnesses with any common pair outside $\mathcal{R}$ or with nonequivalent exchanges. There are at most $v^3$ common pairs and at most $H$ roles per witness, so total deletion is at most \refstepcounter{equation}\label{ent:cleaning-loss}$[q_0+(2H+1)\xi]v^5\le{}L^{-249}v^5$ \textup{(\ref{ent:cleaning-loss})}. No host edge is deleted from other witness positions.

Twice applying Markov to Lemma~\ref{rpe:rich} gives all but $o_{\epsilon\to0}(1)D$ colours with designated edge sets $E_c$ of size $(1-o_{\epsilon\to0}(1))m$, each oriented edge having at most $o_{\epsilon\to0}(1)m$ deficient partners. Root reversal gives both orientations; \eqref{rpe:rectangle} absorbs the side difference.
Call an edge good if two thirds of $E_c$ are its $\mathcal{R}$-neighbours. Each nongood edge has at least $v/16$ rich nonneighbours; each such pair has original mass at least $b_0v^2$ and contributes half that much nonequivalent mass by the preceding argument. Double counting
bounds nongood edges by $1000H\xi{}v^2/b_0$. Discard colours with more than $\zeta{}v$ of them, and remove the remaining nongood roots. Two retained roots have linearly many common $\mathcal{R}$-neighbours. Choose one avoiding forbidden physical vertices, then compose two
switchers with disjoint interiors and palettes. The intermediate root
enters the interior; its two odd paths close to an even cycle.
External paths remain separate. The resulting order is at most $2(H+\ell)$ with forbidden allowance $\beta{}v/4$.

Discard colours losing more than $\tau{}v^4$ in cleaning, at most $L^{-79}v$ colours. Taking $\epsilon$ small yields the promised candidate counts and $E_c^0$. Restrict only original roots to $E_c^0$. Choose the initial fixed entropy loss so that each retained colour
loses at most $\chi{}m$ original root edges, where $\chi\le\min(\eta/4,\kappa/100)$, and its deficient-pair fraction is at most $1/8$. The uncleaned rich-root mass is at least $\kappa{}v^2D^2/(3P)$ for large $v$. Restricting both original roots to $E_c^0$ loses at most $4\chi{}mvD^2/P$, by the unmarked root-edge load; a fixed positive fraction remains. Cleaning costs
at most $\tau{}v^4$. For any local query, filter its physical forbidden set and collective $B$ using the fixed-root-colour loads; this costs $O((\beta+\theta)v^4)$. Relative to $v^2D^2/P$ these last two losses are $O((\tau+\beta+\theta)P/p_*^2)=o(1)$. Thus \eqref{rpe:surviving-mass} holds with fixed positive $\kappa'$ \emph{before} applying the hold-mask extraction.

\subsection{Local buffers and the exact template ledger}
\label{ent:local-subsection}
\begin{proof}[Completion of Theorem~\ref{rpe:theorem}]
First build a local absorber for 100 fresh roots. Include $c$ in the initial forbidden palette. The filtered fan has a fixed first cycle
and common word, and polynomially many disjoint partner cycles, all
original roots in $E_c^0$ and all common pairs in $\mathcal{R}$. Write $h=2k$. On each cycle remove its original root from the odd matching, leaving $T_*,T_i$ of size $k-1$ with the same palette. Even matching palettes are $A\cup\{a_*\},A\cup\{a_i\}$, $|A|=k-1$. Select 100 partners with one exchange colour, or 100 with distinct exchanges different from $a_*$. Otherwise at most
100 colours, each appearing at most 99 times, exhaust the fan.

In the distinct case choose a disjoint buffer switching $a_i,a_*$ for each $i$, of order $s_i\le\ell$. Its common palette avoids all cycle and earlier buffer colours; repeated $a_*$ is permitted only as an exchange endpoint. For each corresponding edge pair in $T_*,T_i$ choose a separated $\mathcal{R}$-switcher. All interiors avoid every cycle, buffer and earlier interior; their entire output
palettes avoid all cycle colours (including their own root colour),
all buffer colours and earlier output palettes. These successive
queries use \refstepcounter{equation}\label{ent:local-budget}$O(H(H+\ell))\le{}L^{76}<L_0=o(\beta{}v)$ \textup{(\ref{ent:local-budget})} resources. Collective exceptions were chosen before these queries.

Let $Q$ be the sum of the $100(k-1)$ terminal-switch orders and $S=\sum_is_i$, taking $S=0$ in the equal-exchange case. The interior contains all $V(T_*),V(T_i)$, terminal-switch interiors and buffers. For input $i$, bundle $i$ covers $T_*$, other bundles cover their $T_j$, and cycle $i$'s even matching covers its root and $T_i$. Buffer $i$ uses $a_*$, the others use $a_j$, so the exchange palette is always $\{a_*,a_1,\ldots,a_{100}\}$. Buffer common palettes total $S-100$. Thus \refstepcounter{equation}\label{ent:buffer-count}$|W|=2(k-1)+2Q+2S,|C|=k+Q+S,|W\cap{}X|=|W\cap{}Y|=|C|-1$ \textup{(\ref{ent:buffer-count})}. For equal exchanges no buffers are needed and the same identities
hold with $S=0$. No root endpoint belongs to the common interior.

Extend the prescribed admissible $E$ to an admissible set of
100 roots in $E_c^0$; each excluded physical vertex rules out at most two matching edges,
so the linear pool suffices. Build the fresh absorber outside them.
Attach corresponding prescribed and fresh roots by the robust
order-$2(H+\ell)$ separated switchers, choosing disjoint interiors and palettes outside all roots, the fresh absorber and initial
resources. Include all fresh root endpoints in the new interior.
For a selected prescribed root its attachment covers that root;
all other attachments cover their fresh roots; the fresh absorber
covers the remaining fresh root. If its palette size was $q$ and attachment orders sum to $q'$, the new interior size is $(2q-2)+200+\sum_i(2s_i-2)=2(q+q')-2$. Each side has $q+q'-1$ vertices. The order is at most $L^{80}$, proving the local assertion.

For the larger absorber put $m_1=|E_1|$ and $s=3g$. Extend $E_1$ to an admissible $\widehat{E}$ of size $3s+m_1-g=8g+m_1\le11g$, choosing each added root outside the forbidden physical set and
all labels of earlier roots. A forbidden label excludes at most
two edges of this matching, so its linear pool permits the $O(g)$ greedy choices. Partition it as $E_Y\cup{}E_Z$ with $|E_Y|=2s=6g$, $E_1\subseteq{}E_Z$ and $|E_Z|=s+m_1-g\in[s,2s]$. The distributive template
\cite[Lemma 2.1]{Montgomery} has left side $T$ of size $3s=9g$, right sides of size $2s,2s$, maximum degree 100, and matches $T$ to the first right side together with any $s$-subset of the second. Delete excess second-side vertices and identify the remainder with $E_Y,E_Z$; the required matching property persists.

At each $x\in{}T$ build the local absorber for its nonempty neighbourhood, with disjoint interiors $W_x$ and palettes $C_x$, avoiding all $\widehat{E}$ vertices, initial resources and $c$. The physical budget is at most $\beta{}m/100+22g+18gL^{80}<\beta{}v/100$; the palette budget is smaller. Put $W=V(\widehat{E}\setminus{}E_1)\cup\bigcup_xW_x$ and $C=\bigcup_xC_x$. Exactly $8g$ auxiliary roots enter the interior, while there are $9g$ local absorbers. Therefore $|W\cap{}X|=|W\cap{}Y|=8g+\sum_x(|C_x|-1)=|C|-g$ and $|C|\le9gL^{80}=o(b)$. For $E'\subset{}E_1$, $|E'|=g$, the $9g$ roots $(\widehat{E}\setminus{}E_1)\cup{}E'$ consist of $E_Y$ and an $s$-subset of $E_Z$. A template matching assigns one to each local absorber; their disjoint-palette matchings cover exactly $W\cup{}V(E')$.

Finally add disjoint edges in the prescribed missing colours, then
fresh colours until the palette size is $b$. Prescribed matchings have size $\kappa_0m$. At least $(1-2\delta)D$ other colours have matching size at least $m/2$, since total missing incidences on the larger side are at most $\delta{}Dm$. All protected vertices total $o(m)$, each excluding at most two edges of a colour matching; thus every greedy choice is possible. Each padding edge adds one
colour and one interior vertex per side. The final counts are
exactly $b,b-g,b-g$, and all physical and palette avoidance persists.
\end{proof}
