\section{Real connectors, reservoir routing, and integer quotas}
\label{cyc:section}\label{period:router-section}
We first prove the routing input independently of local absorption. Matrix
norms are operator norms; rows index tails and columns index heads. A
resource is a physical vertex or a colour. In a connector family the
endpoints are anchored: disjointness concerns interiors and palettes.

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-error walk count]\label{rt:lem:walks}
Fix $p_0>0$ and $\rho<1$. There are an integer $L\ge4$ and $\varepsilon_*>0$ such that, if a simple digraph on $m$ vertices has row and column sums in $[D-\varepsilon{}m,D+\varepsilon{}m]$, $D\ge{}p_0m$, $\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_*$, and $\|A-(D/m)J\|\le\rho{}D$, then $(A^k)_{xy}\ge{}D^k/(2m)$ for every $x,y$ and $k=L,L+1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $\mu=(1+\rho)/2$; choose $L$ with $\mu^{L-2}\le{}p_0/8$ and $\varepsilon_*\le\min(1/18,p_0(1-\rho)/20,p_0/(36(L+1)))$. For $\varepsilon>0$, set $D'=D+2\varepsilon{}m$, let $a_i,b_j$ be the row and column deficits to $D'$, and $s=\sum{}a_i=\sum{}b_j$. The matrix $B_{ij}=a_ib_j/s$ has these deficits, $0\le{}B_{ij}\le9\varepsilon$, and $\|B\|\le3\varepsilon{}m$. Thus $W=A+B$ is $D'$-regular, with entries at most $1+9\varepsilon$. For $E=W-D'J/m$ we have $EJ=JE=0$, $\|E\|\le\rho{}D+5\varepsilon{}m\le\mu{}D'$, and every row and column of $E$ has squared norm at most $2D'$. For $k=L,L+1$, $|(E^k)_{xy}|\le2(D')^{k-1}\mu^{k-2}\le(D')^k/(4m)$. The exact identity $W^k-A^k=\sum_{j<k}A^jBW^{k-1-j}$ bounds each entry of the difference by $9k\varepsilon(D')^{k-1}\le(D')^k/(4m)$. Subtract this from $W^k=(D')^kJ/m+E^k$. If $\varepsilon=0$ take $B=0,D'=D$. Artificial weights have been used only for counting.
\end{proof}

Here is the corresponding statement for approximate incidence blocks.
Its density need not be at most one half.
For the power-gap lemma, let $G$ be loopless, simple and properly coloured with $D=pn+o(n)$ colours, $p>0$. Suppose $V=\bigsqcup{}A_i=\bigsqcup{}B_i$, with finitely many parts of sizes $|A_i|,|B_i|=a_in+o(n)$, $a_i>0$. All real arcs go from $A_i$ to $B_i$; every vertex has in/outdegree $D-o(n)$, and each colour has $a_in-o(n)$ arcs in block $i$. Its zero-padded real matrix satisfies $\|Q_i-(D/m_i)J\|\le\rho{}D$, where $m_i=\max(|A_i|,|B_i|)$ and $\rho<1$. Let $|B_i\cap{}A_j|/n\to{}c_{ij}$, and assume the circulation $C=(c_{ij})$ has strongly connected support of period $r$.

\begin{lemma}[Conditional power gap and real connectors]\label{rou:power}\label{pq:comparison}\label{pq:power-gap}\label{periodic:power-gap}\label{pq:connectors}\label{periodic:connectors}
After deleting zero-limit cells and equalizing physical phases by $o(n)$ deletions, there is a fixed multiple $H\ge10r$ of $r$ such that every distinct compatible pair $x\in{}Z_a$, $y\in{}Z_{a+L\pmod{}r}$ has a positive linear family of real rainbow $L$-arc paths for each $H-1\le{}L\le{}H+r-1$. Interiors and palettes are pairwise disjoint. The families tolerate sufficiently small linear forbidden sets, uniformly over endpoints and lengths. For $r=1$ there is no phase restriction.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The cyclic classes $I_0,\ldots,I_{r-1}$ of the quotient define $Z_a=\bigcup_{i\in{}I_a}A_i=\bigcup_{i\in{}I_{a-1}}B_i$ after the zero cells are deleted. Every real arc advances a phase. Conservation
of $C$ gives phase masses $1/r$; hence equalization costs $o(n)$, retaining any specified root in a positive cell. Degree, colour, and
norm errors remain $o(n)$. Rename the remaining order $n$; its limiting proportions are unchanged, and the phase sizes are now
exactly $n/r$.

Extend every partial colour matching to a permutation, phase by phase
if $r>1$, and let $\widetilde{A}=A+B$ be their sum. Every row and column sum of $B$ is $o(n)$, so $\|B\|=o(n)$ and $T=\widetilde{A}/D$ is doubly stochastic. Fix one completed permutation $P_0$ and, only for this calculation, replace $B_i$ by $B'_i=P_0(A_i)$. Almost fullness gives $|B'_i\triangle{}B_i|=o(n)$ and $|B'_i|=|A_i|=:m_i'$. Let $V_A,V_B$ have normalized indicator columns of these equal-sized parts, put $P_A=V_AV_A^{\mathsf{T}}$, $P_B=V_BV_B^{\mathsf{T}}$, and $S=V_AV_B^{\mathsf{T}}$. Let $T_0$ retain only real entries $A_i\to{}B'_i$, divided by $D$. Moving $o(n)$ head labels affects $o(n^2)$ entries; their Frobenius cost after normalization is $o(1)$. Thus $\|T-T_0\|=o(1),\|T_0P_B-S\|+\|P_AT_0-S\|=o(1),\|(I-P_A)T_0(I-P_B)\|\le\rho+o(1)$ \textup{(RQ.1)}. For the two averaging estimates, row deficits are uniformly $o(1)$; column deficits are $o(1)$ except in $o(n)$ columns and are bounded everywhere, so their squared sum is $o(n)$. The last estimate follows from the given padded block bounds and the same Frobenius perturbation.
Set $E=(I-P_A)T_0(I-P_B)$ and $T_*=S+E$. Then $\|T-T_*\|=o(1)$. The summands have orthogonal domains and ranges; hence $T_*$ is a contraction, fixes constants on both sides, and acts on phase-constant
vectors by the cyclic shift. It need not be nonnegative.

For completeness, the finite quotient gives a uniform power gap.
Put $M=V_B^{\mathsf{T}}V_A$ and $w_i=\sqrt{m_i'/n}$. The stochastic matrix similar to $M$ has entries $|B'_i\cap{}A_j|/m_i'$. Entries on the limiting support are bounded below by a fixed $\theta>0$. Choose $q\equiv-1\pmod{}r$ so that every pair in the compatible cyclic classes has a length-$q$ walk. Such a fixed $q$ exists by primitivity within each cyclic class. Write $w^{(a)}$ for the unit vector equal to $\sqrt{r}w_i$ on $I_a$ and zero elsewhere. For $N_q=\sum_aw^{(a)}(w^{(a-1)})^{\mathsf{T}}$, a fixed $\delta>0$ satisfies $M^q\ge\delta{}N_q$ entrywise. Both matrices fix $w$ on both sides. The nonnegative remainder $(M^q-\delta{}N_q)/(1-\delta)$ has norm at most one: weighted Cauchy--Schwarz gives $|(Uz)_i|^2\le{}w_i\sum_jU_{ij}|z_j|^2/w_j$, and summation uses $U^{\mathsf{T}}w=w$. The case $\delta=1$ is immediate. Therefore, with $h=q+1$, $S^h$ contracts the orthogonal complement of the physical phase-constant space by $1-\delta$.

Let $P$ be its projection, and replace $\rho$ by a slightly larger fixed number below one. Orthogonality gives $\|T_*f\|^2\le\|f\|^2-(1-\rho^2)\|(I-P_B)f\|^2$. For unit $f\perp\operatorname{im}P$, put $e=1-\|T_*^hf\|^2$. Telescoping this energy loss and then the recursion $T_*x=Sx+Ex$ gives $\|T_*^hf-S^hf\|\le\sqrt{he/(1-\rho^2)}$. Thus $\sqrt{1-e}\le1-\delta+\sqrt{he/(1-\rho^2)}$, implying $e\ge(1-\rho^2)\delta^2/(4h)$. The $o(1)$ perturbation transfers this to $T$: for a fixed $\sigma<1$, $\|T^h-P\|\le\sigma$ and $\|T^b(I-P)\|\le\sigma^{\lfloor{}b/h\rfloor}$.

Use real end factors to count $A\widetilde{A}^{L-2}A$. For compatible $x,y$ its phase-constant term is $(1-o(1))rD^L/n$; its remainder is at most $D^{L-1}\sigma^{\lfloor(L-2)/h\rfloor}$ because real end rows and columns have norm at most $\sqrt{D}$. Choose the fixed $H$ so the error is smaller than $pD^{L-1}/4$ for the entire indicated length interval. Divide by $D^{L-1}$ and interpret the expression as a uniform $(L-1)$-colour word followed by a tested real last arc. At every state only $o(D)$ choices are artificial. Rejecting at the first artificial transition therefore loses $o(1)$ probability; the first and last arcs were already real. There remain $\Omega(n^{L-1})$ real walks.

A specified repeated vertex determines one internal coordinate.
For a specified repeated colour on nonadjacent arcs, expose all but
an internal endpoint of one arc; incoming or outgoing properness
determines it. For adjacent arcs expose an internal outer endpoint
of their two-edge segment, which exists since $L\ge3$. Thus all repetitions cost $O_L(n^{L-2})$ walks. A specified internal vertex or colour also occurs in $O_L(n^{L-2})$ candidates. Greedily delete used or forbidden resources from the remaining $\Omega(n^{L-1})$ candidates, obtaining a fixed positive linear disjoint family.
The same argument applied to Lemma~\ref{rt:lem:walks} proves the
one-block fixed-error connector statement. No completed edge is
ever selected as a connector resource.
\end{proof}

A Hamilton router from $A$ to $B$ has a spanning partition into directed $A$-to-$B$ paths that joins every external $B$-to-$A$ path system into one cycle; all partitions use the same full rainbow palette. Assume the preceding real connector families, proper colouring with $O(n)$ colours and positive linear minimum semidegree. Independently sample vertices and colours into reservoirs at fixed positive rates. Endpoint sets are disjoint host sets, and $a,M$ below are integers. In period $r>1$, require $r\mid{}a,M$ and $|A\cap{}Z_j|=|B\cap{}Z_j|=a/r$.

\begin{lemma}[A simultaneous router, including mixed phases]\label{rt:thm:router}\label{sg:lem:router}\label{period:mixed-router}
There are $q_0,w,c>0$ such that, with probability $1-\exp(-cn)$, every endpoint query $|A|=|B|=a$, $2\le{}a\le{}q_0n$, and $10Ha\le{}M\le20Ha$ admits a Hamilton router with exactly $M$ internal reservoir vertices and $M+a$ reservoir colours. It avoids any $wn$ forbidden resources except named endpoints and survives $o(n)$ role changes. In period $r>1$, each phase contains exactly $M/r$ internal vertices.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We detail the comparator construction, so the router requires no
unproved routing primitive. Use source pairs in $Z_0$ and heads in $Z_1$ when $r>1$; for $r=1$, a random balanced partition into source and head sets leaves linear indegree at every head. Make an auxiliary
bipartite graph between ordered pairs of distinct sources and ordered
pairs of distinct colours. Join $(x_1,x_2)$ to $(a,b)$ with auxiliary colour $y$ if $x_1y,x_2y$ have colours $a,b$. The graph is simple, properly coloured, has $O(n^2)$ vertices and $\Omega(n^3)$ edges, also after sufficiently small linear resource exclusions.
Relate tuples sharing an original coordinate. For any tuple and
another vertex, at most four neighbours of that vertex are related
to the tuple: a shared coordinate fixes the auxiliary colour and
the other coordinate. Equality of auxiliary colours has local bound
one. The compatible-cycle theorem \cite[Theorem 3.4]{BMMP}, with $k=3,s=4$, applies since its threshold is $O(n^{8/3})$.

The compatible six-cycle gives six distinct sources $u_1,u_2$, $v_1,v_2$ and $w_1,w_2$, and six distinct heads $u_3,u_4$, $v_3,v_4$ and $w_3,w_4$. Its six colours are distinct. The incoming colour pairs at $u_3,u_4$ are $(e_1,e_2)$ and $(c_1,c_2)$; at $v_3,v_4$ they are $(c_1,c_2)$ and $(d_1,d_2)$; at $w_3,w_4$ they are $(d_1,d_2)$ and $(e_1,e_2)$. Connect $u_3\to{}v_1$, $u_4\to{}v_2$, $v_3\to{}w_1$ and $v_4\to{}w_2$ by disjoint fresh $(H-1)$-arc connectors. Their residues are correct. Recording only the six-cycle vertices, the straight state uses $u_1u_3v_1v_3w_1w_3$ and $u_2u_4v_2v_4w_2w_4$; the crossed state uses $u_1u_4v_2v_3w_1w_4$ and $u_2u_3v_1v_4w_2w_3$. Each transition between a head and a source denotes its connector.
Both states span the same $4H+4$ vertices and use the same $4H+2$ colours once each; each constituent path has length $2H+1$. Greedy repetition supplies linearly many vertex- and colour-disjoint
comparators. Fix this family and all connector families before sampling.
A comparator survives with probability $\lambda_V^{4H+4}\lambda_C^{4H+2}$, and an $L$-connector with $\lambda_V^{L-1}\lambda_C^L$. Events within a family are independent. Chernoff and a union bound over $O(n^2)$ families leave a linear supply in every family. One changed resource destroys at most one
member per family; hence all $o(n)$ repairs preserve this event.

Use four rows of $a$ column vertices, rows one and three in $Z_0$ and rows two and four in $Z_1$. Put comparator gates on odd adjacent column pairs between the first two rows and on even adjacent pairs
between the last two. Join each middle column by an $(H-1)$-connector; fill the two unmatched gate positions with single arcs. There are $a-1$ comparators. The central vertex count is $4a+4H(a-1)+(H-2)a=(5H+2)a-4H$. Attach $x\in{}A\cap{}Z_j$ to row one by a connector of length $H+[-j]_r$, and row four to $y\in{}B\cap{}Z_j$ by one of length $H+[j-1]_r$, with residues chosen in $\{0,\ldots,r-1\}$. For $r=1$ these lengths are $H$. Uniform endpoint profiles give the internal count $M_0=H(7a-4)+(r-1)a$ \textup{(MP.1)}. It is divisible by $r$ and less than $10Ha$. Increase one middle connector by $z=M-M_0$ arcs: take $z$ fresh greedy steps, then an $(H-1)$-connector to its reserved endpoint. Since $r\mid{}z$, the final connector has the correct phase. Each greedy step uses a first
edge from a surviving length-$H$ family; these first neighbours and colours are distinct, giving linear choice. Single gate arcs are
obtained by the same first-edge rule. Choose every structure while
avoiding all other external endpoints, previous resources, and the
forbidden set. The total demand $O_H(a)+O(wn)$ is below the supply when $q_0,w$ are small. The construction therefore works for adaptively chosen endpoints, even those originally in the reservoir.

To verify the Hamilton property, index the external bijection by a
permutation $\tau$. With all gates straight its cycles are those of $\tau$. Contract these cycles in the path of adjacent column indices and choose a spanning tree. Turn on its gates. In the actual product
order (one gate round followed by the other), each selected tree edge
joins different unions of original cycles, so its transposition merges
two current cycles. The final permutation is one cycle. Palette and
vertex sets are independent of gate states. A spanning partition into $a$ paths on $M+2a$ vertices uses $M+a$ arcs. Finally, writing $I_j$ for its internal phase counts, arc balance gives $I_j+|A\cap{}Z_j|=I_{j+1}+|B\cap{}Z_{j+1}|$; hence $I_j=M/r$. Everything used one simultaneous supply event, without a union bound
over adaptive choices.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact integer circulation quotas]\label{pq:quotas}
For a fixed strongly connected support $Q$ of period $r$, a real circulation $x$ with integer total $v\in{}r\mathbb{Z}$ and all supported entries sufficiently large has an integer circulation $Z$ on the same support, of total $v$, with $Z=x+O_Q(1)$ entrywise.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The circulation with lower bounds $\lfloor{}x_e\rfloor$ and upper bounds $\lceil{}x_e\rceil$ has an integral point $Z_0$: after subtracting lower bounds, integral augmenting paths route the integer
vertex deficits through capacity-one edges, since the fractional
residual is feasible. Thus $\|Z_0-x\|_\infty\le1$ and its total differs from $v$ by at most $|E(Q)|$. Simple directed cycle lengths have gcd $r$; Bezout supplies a signed integer circulation $W$ of total $r$. The total of $Z_0$ is divisible by $r$, by its cycle decomposition. Therefore $Z=Z_0+(v-\sum_e(Z_0)_e)W/r$ has the required total and bounded entrywise error. Taking all $x_e$ above this fixed error makes $Z\ge0$. Every circulation on this support has equal mass in the cyclic classes, so $Z$ has exactly $v/r$ vertices per physical phase.
\end{proof}
