Kaneko, Kano and Suzuki conjectured that every properly edge-coloured complete graph $K_n$ contains $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ edge-disjoint rainbow spanning trees; for even $n$ these must decompose the edges.
Montgomery, Pokrovskiy and Sudakov conjectured a generalisation to rainbow forests: every properly coloured graph with $km$ edges and at most $m$ edges of each colour should contain $(1-o(1))m$ edge-disjoint rainbow forests with $k$ edges each, and Xu recently proved this for $k\le 2m$.
We show that this holds without the assumption that the colouring is proper.
In particular, every colouring of $K_n$ with at most $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ edges of each colour contains $(1-o(1))n/2$ edge-disjoint rainbow spanning trees, answering a question of Glock, K\"uhn, Montgomery and Osthus; for complete graphs, we find all but $O(\log^2 n)$ of the trees.
Finally, we prove the Kaneko--Kano--Suzuki conjecture for large $n$ when every colour class has at most $n/2-Cn^{2/3}(\log n)^{2/3}$ edges.
